Under the hood

Solvers and optimizer built from first principles

No commercial CFD underneath: every method below is implemented in this codebase, tested, and checked against published data.

GPU lattice-Boltzmann CFD

D3Q19 regularized collision with a Smagorinsky large-eddy model and curved-wall (Bouzidi) boundaries. The CUDA and CPU kernels are generated from one source and agree to 0.2 %; on a laptop GTX 1650 Ti the tunnel runs at about 400 million cell updates a second.

9 s 3-angle rocket sweep vs 56 s on 6 CPU cores

An optimizer that knows its own error

Before searching, it re-runs the baseline shifted by a fraction of a cell to measure the solver's noise, ranks every named parameter in a screening pass, then searches the strongest ones with a Gaussian-process model. Gains smaller than twice the measured error are not reported.

−7.7 % supersonic rocket drag 28 designs in 30 s

Wings at fixed lift

Vortex-lattice lifting surfaces with NACA 4- and 5-digit sections, viscous section polars from a neural surrogate (NeuralFoil), stall and stability margins, and a fuselage drag model — so a wing is never "improved" by simply lifting less.

−15.8 % wing drag at fixed lift NACA 2412 → 2408

Supersonic to Mach 12

Second-order shock-expansion theory along each meridian, modified Newtonian flow, Fay–Riddell stagnation heating and radiative-equilibrium skin temperature checked against each material's service limit.

±7 % ogive drag, Mach 1.5–4 vs published data

Propellers in the loop

Blade-element momentum theory for each propeller, injected into the flow as an actuator disk with thrust and swirl, so the airframe is tested in its own slipstream.

Questions

Which tunnel should I use?

Anything with a propeller that matters for its drag: the RC tunnel. Rockets, UAVs, pods and fairings up to Mach 1.2: the General tunnel. Rockets, missiles, re-entry bodies and high-speed aircraft above Mach 1.2: the Supersonic tunnel. A project can switch tunnels at any time.

How is the transonic range handled?

The General tunnel's CFD is computed at low Mach number and corrected for compressibility (Kármán–Tsien) face by face, which is sound up to the body's critical Mach number — where the flow first goes sonic, typically Mach 0.7–0.85 — and that number is reported for every body. Above it, shocks form; the drag rise is estimated by bridging the corrected CFD to the supersonic surface methods at Mach 1.2. That band is an estimate and is labelled as one in every result: a true transonic answer needs a compressible flow solver.

How are propellers simulated?

Each propeller's thrust and torque come from blade-element momentum theory using its diameter, pitch, blade count and rpm, checked against UIUC wind-tunnel data for APC propellers. That loading is applied to the flow as an actuator disk, so the slipstream and its swirl wash over the airframe. Blades are not resolved one by one.

How hot can it get?

The Supersonic tunnel runs to Mach 12 and 80 km. Above Mach 6 real-gas chemistry starts to matter and above Mach 8 it is significant; the methods use a perfect gas, so temperatures there are flagged as approximate.

What is a compute credit?

One draft CFD run costs 1 credit (standard 3, high 8). In the General tunnel a run is one angle of attack; with propellers turning it is one angle and one airspeed. A whole supersonic sweep costs 1 credit, and supersonic optimizations 1 credit per ten designs.

Is the Lite plan really free?

Yes: all three tunnels, 40 compute credits a month, one propeller, short optimizations, no card required.

Who owns my designs?

You do. Models and results are private to your account and deleted when you delete the project or your account.
